# Lesson 13 — Growth vs. Value Investing

> *"A good company and a good investment are not always the same thing."*

---

# Introduction

In the previous lesson, we learned about market capitalization.

Market capitalization helps us understand the size of a company. A large-cap company is usually more mature, while a small-cap company may have more growth potential but also more risk.

However, company size is not the only way to understand stocks.

Two companies may both be large-cap stocks, but investors may view them very differently.

One company may be growing quickly. Another company may grow slowly, but its stock price may look cheap.

This leads to two important investment styles:

**growth investing** and **value investing**.

Growth investors focus on future growth. Value investors focus on price and value.

Both styles are useful. Both also have risks.

For beginners, the goal is not to choose one side immediately. The goal is to understand how different investors think.

---

## Why Investors Need Different Styles

When investors buy a stock, they are not only buying the company today.

They are also buying expectations about the future.

If investors believe a company will grow quickly, they may be willing to pay a high price for its stock.

If investors believe a company is boring, slow, or facing problems, they may only be willing to pay a low price.

But investors are not always correct.

Sometimes they become too excited about a fast-growing company.

Sometimes they become too pessimistic about a stable company.

Growth investing and value investing are two ways to think about this problem.

Growth investing asks:

**Which companies can become much bigger in the future?**

Value investing asks:

**Which companies are cheaper than they should be?**

Both questions are important.

---

## What Is a Growth Company?

A **growth company** is a company that is expected to grow faster than the average company.

This growth may come from higher revenue, higher profits, more customers, or a larger market share.

Growth companies are often found in industries such as technology, artificial intelligence, cloud computing, electric vehicles, biotechnology, or digital services.

Examples may include companies such as NVIDIA, Tesla, Amazon in its earlier years, or Tencent during its faster growth period.

These companies attract attention because investors believe their future may be much larger than their present.

A growth company often reinvests money into the business.

Instead of paying large dividends, it may spend money on research, new products, new factories, new employees, or international expansion.

The company is trying to become bigger.

Investors accept this because they hope future profits will be much higher.

---

## What Is Growth Investing?

**Growth investing** means investing in companies that are expected to grow faster than the market.

A growth investor is willing to pay a higher price today because they believe the company will become much more valuable in the future.

For example, a company may not earn much profit today, but its revenue may be growing quickly every year.

A value investor may say:

"This stock is too expensive."

But a growth investor may say:

"The company is still young. Its future profit may be much larger."

Growth investing is based on future expectations.

The key question is not only:

"Is this company profitable today?"

The key question is:

"How large can this company become in the future?"

This is why growth stocks often have high valuations.

Investors are paying for expected future growth.

---

## Advantages of Growth Investing

The main advantage of growth investing is the possibility of high returns.

If a company grows successfully for many years, its stock price may rise greatly.

Some of the best-performing stocks in history were growth companies at the beginning.

They entered large markets, created new products, and became much bigger over time.

Growth investing can also help investors participate in major economic changes.

For example, the rise of the internet, smartphones, cloud computing, and artificial intelligence created many growth opportunities.

A successful growth company can change an industry.

It can also create large wealth for long-term shareholders.

This is why growth stocks often receive so much attention.

They represent possibility.

---

## Risks of Growth Investing

The biggest risk of growth investing is that expectations may become too high.

A growth stock can be a good company, but still be a bad investment if the price is too expensive.

This is very important.

If investors already expect perfect results, even a small disappointment can cause the stock price to fall sharply.

Growth stocks are often sensitive to news.

If revenue growth slows, profits disappoint, or competition becomes stronger, the stock price may fall quickly.

Another risk is uncertainty.

Many growth companies are still developing. Their business models may not be fully proven. They may need large amounts of capital. They may also face strong competitors.

Growth investing can be rewarding, but it requires patience and emotional control.

---

## What Is a Value Company?

A **value company** is a company whose stock price appears low compared with its business value.

It may not be growing very quickly.

It may be in a mature industry.

It may be temporarily unpopular.

But it may still have real profits, assets, cash flow, or dividends.

Value companies are often found in industries such as banking, insurance, energy, utilities, telecommunications, industrials, or consumer staples.

These companies may not sound exciting.

They may not appear often in technology headlines.

But they can still be important businesses.

A value company may be cheap because investors are pessimistic.

Sometimes the pessimism is correct.

Sometimes it is too extreme.

Value investors try to understand the difference.

---

## What Is Value Investing?

**Value investing** means investing in companies whose stock prices seem lower than their real value.

A value investor is not mainly looking for the fastest growth.

They are looking for a good business at a reasonable or cheap price.

The basic idea is simple:

**buy something for less than it is worth.**

For example, a stable company may have real profits, strong assets, and regular dividends.

But because investors dislike its industry, the stock price may be low.

A value investor may think:

"The market is too pessimistic. This company is worth more than the current price."

Value investing often uses valuation measures, such as price-to-earnings ratio, price-to-book ratio, dividend yield, or cash flow.

We will study valuation in more detail later.

For now, the key idea is simple:

value investors care deeply about price.

---

## Advantages of Value Investing

The main advantage of value investing is its focus on risk control.

Value investors do not want to overpay.

They understand that even a good company can be a poor investment at the wrong price.

By buying at a lower price, they hope to create a margin of safety.

A **margin of safety** means some protection if the future is not perfect.

Value investing can also be easier to understand for some beginners.

Many value companies have real profits, real assets, and dividends.

These businesses may be less exciting, but they may be more stable.

Value investing also teaches discipline.

It reminds investors not to follow market excitement blindly.

When everyone loves a stock, the price may already be too high.

When everyone hates a stock, there may be an opportunity.

---

## Risks of Value Investing

Value investing also has risks.

The biggest risk is called a **value trap**.

A value trap is a stock that looks cheap, but is cheap for a good reason.

For example, a company may have a low price-to-earnings ratio, but its profits may be falling.

Its industry may be in long-term decline.

Its debt may be too high.

Its management may be poor.

In this case, the stock is not truly undervalued.

It is simply a weak business.

Another risk is patience.

Value stocks can remain unpopular for a long time.

Even if the investor is correct, the market may take years to recognize the value.

During that time, other stocks may rise faster.

This can make value investing emotionally difficult.

Value investing is not just buying cheap stocks.

It requires understanding why the stock is cheap.

---

## Growth vs. Value: The Main Difference

The difference between growth and value investing is mainly about emphasis.

Growth investing focuses more on future growth.

Value investing focuses more on current price compared with business value.

A growth investor may accept a high price if future growth is strong enough.

A value investor may avoid a high price even if the company is excellent.

A growth investor often asks:

"How much can this company grow?"

A value investor often asks:

"Am I paying too much?"

Both questions are useful.

A great investment often needs both:

a good business and a reasonable price.

This is why growth and value are not complete opposites.

In real life, good investors often think about both.

---

## Can a Stock Be Both Growth and Value?

Yes.

A company can have growth potential and still be reasonably priced.

This is often what investors hope to find.

For example, a company may be growing steadily, but the market may not fully recognize its future potential.

In that case, the stock may be both a growth opportunity and a value opportunity.

However, such opportunities are not easy to find.

If a company is obviously excellent, many investors may already know it.

The stock price may already be high.

This is why investing is difficult.

It is not enough to find a good company.

You also need to consider the price.

A good company and a good investment are related, but they are not always the same thing.

---

## Growth and Value in ETFs

Many investors do not choose individual growth or value stocks.

Instead, they use ETFs.

There are ETFs that focus on growth stocks.

There are also ETFs that focus on value stocks.

A growth ETF may include companies with high revenue growth, high expected earnings growth, or strong market momentum.

A value ETF may include companies with lower valuation ratios, higher dividends, or more stable current profits.

However, beginners do not need to choose these immediately.

A broad market ETF already includes both growth and value companies.

For example, an ETF tracking the S&P 500 includes large growth companies and mature value companies.

This is one reason broad index investing is suitable for many beginners.

It avoids the need to predict which style will perform better.

---

## Which Style Is Better for Beginners?

For most beginners, the best starting point is not to choose between growth and value.

The better starting point is to understand both.

Growth investing teaches us to think about the future.

Value investing teaches us to care about price.

A good long-term investor needs both lessons.

If you only think about growth, you may overpay.

If you only think about cheap prices, you may buy weak companies.

For a beginner, a broad market ETF can be a simple solution.

It allows you to own many companies across different styles.

As your knowledge grows, you can learn more about specific investment styles.

But your first goal should be building a strong foundation, not making complicated decisions too early.

---

## Summary

Growth investing and value investing are two important ways to think about stocks.

Growth investing focuses on companies that may grow faster than the market.

These companies may offer high potential returns, but their prices often include high expectations.

Value investing focuses on companies that may be underpriced.

These companies may offer a margin of safety, but some cheap stocks are value traps.

Growth investors often ask:

"How big can this company become?"

Value investors often ask:

"Am I paying a reasonable price?"

Both questions matter.

A good company is not always a good investment if the price is too high.

A cheap stock is not always a good investment if the business is weak.

For beginners, broad index investing can provide exposure to both growth and value companies.
