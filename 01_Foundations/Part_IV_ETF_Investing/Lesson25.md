# Lesson 25 — Liquidity

> *"A good investment should not only be worth owning. It should also be practical to buy and sell."*

---

# Introduction

ETF investors often focus on index, return, and fees.

These are important.

But liquidity is also important.

Liquidity means how easily an asset can be bought or sold at a fair price.

For ETFs, liquidity affects trading cost and convenience.

A highly liquid ETF can usually be bought or sold quickly with a small difference between buying and selling prices.

A less liquid ETF may be more expensive to trade.

---

## Trading Volume

Trading volume shows how many shares of an ETF are traded during a period, usually one day.

High trading volume often means many buyers and sellers are active.

This can make trading easier.

Large ETFs tracking major indices often have high volume.

Small or specialized ETFs may trade less often.

Low volume does not always mean an ETF is bad.

But it means investors should be more careful when buying or selling.

---

## Bid-Ask Spread

When you trade an ETF, there is usually a bid price and an ask price.

The bid price is the highest price buyers are currently willing to pay.

The ask price is the lowest price sellers are currently willing to accept.

The difference is called the bid-ask spread.

A narrow spread means the cost of trading is low.

A wide spread means buying and selling may be more expensive.

For long-term investors who trade rarely, this cost may not be as important as the expense ratio.

But it still matters, especially for less liquid ETFs or large trades.

---

## Market Liquidity and ETF Liquidity

ETF liquidity depends on two things.

The first is the liquidity of the ETF shares themselves.

This is shown by trading volume and spreads.

The second is the liquidity of the securities inside the ETF.

If the ETF owns large, actively traded stocks, it is usually easier for the fund structure to support efficient trading.

If the ETF owns small, hard-to-trade securities, liquidity may be weaker.

This is why an ETF's holdings matter.

---

## Practical Habits for Beginners

Beginners do not need to become trading experts.

But they can follow a few simple habits.

Choose broad ETFs with reasonable size and trading activity.

Avoid placing trades during extremely volatile moments if there is no urgent need.

Use limit orders when trading less liquid ETFs, so you control the maximum price you pay or the minimum price you accept.

Do not focus only on the expense ratio.

A very cheap ETF with poor liquidity may still have hidden trading costs.

---

## Summary

Liquidity means how easily an ETF can be bought or sold at a fair price.

Trading volume and bid-ask spread are important signs of ETF liquidity.

The liquidity of the ETF's holdings also matters.

Broad, large, actively traded ETFs are usually easier for beginners to use.
