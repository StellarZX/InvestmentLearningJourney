# Lesson 26 — Dollar-Cost Averaging

> *"Consistency can be more useful than perfect timing."*

---

# Introduction

Many beginners worry about when to invest.

They ask whether the market is too high, whether it will fall soon, or whether they should wait for a better price.

These questions are natural.

But market timing is difficult.

Even professionals cannot consistently predict short-term market movements.

Dollar-cost averaging, often called DCA, is a simple method that helps investors avoid depending on one perfect entry point.

It means investing a fixed amount regularly.

---

## How DCA Works

Imagine an investor puts 1,000 RMB into an ETF every month.

When the ETF price is high, the investor buys fewer shares.

When the ETF price is low, the investor buys more shares.

Over time, the average purchase price becomes a mix of many different market prices.

The investor does not need to decide whether today is the best day.

The plan continues through different market conditions.

This can make investing easier emotionally.

---

## Advantages of DCA

The first advantage is discipline.

Regular investing helps people build wealth from income over time.

The second advantage is emotional control.

Investors are less likely to panic about one purchase price because they know future purchases will continue.

The third advantage is simplicity.

A beginner can follow a DCA plan without reading every market forecast.

The fourth advantage is that DCA works naturally with salaries.

Many people receive income monthly, so monthly investing is practical.

---

## Limitations of DCA

DCA is useful, but it is not perfect.

If the market rises strongly for a long time, investing all available money earlier may produce a higher return than spreading it out.

This is because more money is invested sooner.

DCA also does not protect investors from losses.

If the market falls, the value of earlier purchases can decline.

DCA is not a magic formula.

It is a behavior tool.

---

## Common Misunderstandings

Some people think DCA guarantees profit.

It does not.

The final result still depends on the assets purchased and the long-term performance of the market.

Some people think DCA means buying more when prices fall and stopping when prices rise.

That is not DCA.

DCA means investing regularly according to a plan.

The method is simple, but investors still need to choose suitable assets and understand risk.

---

## Summary

Dollar-cost averaging means investing a fixed amount regularly.

It reduces the need to pick one perfect entry point and helps build discipline.

It can be useful for people investing from monthly income.

However, DCA does not guarantee profit and does not remove market risk.

It is a practical habit, not a perfect strategy.
