# Lesson 16 — Inflation

> *"The number on your bank account matters less than what that money can buy."*

---

# Introduction

In Lesson 15, we learned that compounding can help wealth grow over time.

But growth must be compared with something.

If your money grows by 2% while prices rise by 4%, you are not really richer.

You have more money, but less purchasing power.

This is why investors need to understand inflation.

Inflation directly affects savings, salaries, investment returns, and long-term financial plans.

---

## Purchasing Power

Purchasing power means what your money can buy.

If 100 RMB can buy ten meals today, but only eight meals in the future, the purchasing power of 100 RMB has fallen.

The amount of money is the same.

Its real value is different.

Inflation is the general increase in prices over time.

It does not mean every product rises by the same amount.

But when the overall cost of living increases, each unit of money buys less than before.

---

## Nominal Return and Real Return

Nominal return is the return before adjusting for inflation.

Real return is the return after inflation.

For example, if an investment earns 7% in one year and inflation is 3%, the real return is about 4%.

The exact calculation can be slightly different, but the idea is simple:

real return shows how much your purchasing power has increased.

A high nominal return may look good.

But if inflation is also high, the real result may be much lower.

---

## Why Cash Loses Value

Cash feels safe because its price does not move like stocks.

If you have 10,000 RMB in cash today, you still see 10,000 RMB tomorrow.

This stability is useful for emergency money.

However, cash has a hidden risk.

Over many years, inflation can reduce what that cash can buy.

This does not mean all cash is bad.

Every investor needs some cash for daily life and unexpected events.

The problem appears when all long-term money stays in cash.

---

## Inflation and Long-Term Goals

Inflation is especially important for long-term goals.

Education, housing, healthcare, and retirement can become more expensive over time.

If a person plans only with today's prices, future costs may surprise them.

Investing is not about becoming rich quickly.

It is partly about protecting future purchasing power.

The goal is not only to have more currency units.

The goal is to maintain and grow the real value of wealth.

---

## Summary

Inflation reduces purchasing power.

Nominal return shows how much money increases, while real return shows how much purchasing power increases.

Cash is useful for safety and emergencies, but it may lose real value over long periods.

Long-term investors must think beyond the number in the account.

In the next lesson, we will study risk.

To invest well, we need to understand not only growth and inflation, but also what can go wrong.
