# Lesson 19 — Diversification

> *"A strong portfolio does not depend on one single result."*

---

# Introduction

We have learned that risk cannot be removed completely.

But some risks can be reduced.

Diversification is one of the most important ways to do this.

The idea is simple:

do not put all your money into one company, one industry, or one country.

If one part performs badly, other parts may help balance the result.

Diversification is not exciting.

It does not promise the highest possible return.

But it helps investors survive uncertainty.

---

## Why Diversification Works

No one can predict the future perfectly.

Even famous companies can face problems.

Industries that look strong today may struggle later.

Countries with fast growth can still experience difficult years.

Diversification works because different assets do not always move in the same way.

When one company falls, another may rise.

When one sector is weak, another may be stable.

This does not guarantee profit.

But it reduces dependence on a single outcome.

---

## Company and Sector Risk

Buying one stock means depending heavily on one company.

If the company succeeds, the return may be high.

If the company fails, the loss may also be high.

Even a strong company can face unexpected problems.

Sector risk is similar but broader.

If an investor only buys technology companies, the portfolio depends on the technology sector.

If technology stocks become too expensive or face regulation, the whole portfolio may suffer.

---

## Country Risk

Investing only in one country can also create risk.

Every country has its own economy, currency, interest rates, policies, and market behavior.

Some years, the U.S. market may lead.

Other years, Chinese, European, Japanese, or emerging markets may perform better.

Global diversification can help investors avoid depending completely on one economy.

However, global investing also brings currency and political risks.

Diversification reduces some risks, but it does not remove all risks.

---

## Diversification Through ETFs

ETFs make diversification easier.

Instead of buying many individual stocks, an investor can buy one ETF that owns many companies.

An ETF tracking the S&P 500 gives exposure to hundreds of large U.S. companies.

An ETF tracking the CSI 300 gives exposure to major A-share companies in China.

A global stock ETF may include companies from many countries.

For beginners, broad ETFs can be a practical way to diversify without analyzing every company.

---

## Summary

Diversification means spreading investments across different companies, sectors, countries, or asset types.

It helps reduce unnecessary dependence on one result.

It cannot remove all risk, and it does not guarantee profit.

But it can make a portfolio more stable and easier to hold over time.

In the next lesson, we will study asset allocation.

Diversification asks, "How widely should I spread my investments?"

Asset allocation asks, "How much should I put into each type of asset?"
