# Lesson 38 — Common Beginner Mistakes

> *"Most beginner mistakes are not strange. They are human."*

---

# Introduction

By this point, we have learned many investing principles.

But beginners often make mistakes even after learning the basics.

This is normal.

Investing is simple in theory and difficult in behavior.

The purpose of studying mistakes is not to feel guilty.

It is to recognize common patterns before they become expensive.

Experienced investors are not people who never make mistakes.

They are people who learn from them and build better systems.

---

## Mistake 1: Investing Without an Emergency Fund

Money needed for emergencies should not be invested in volatile assets.

If an unexpected expense appears during a market decline, the investor may be forced to sell at a bad time.

An emergency fund provides safety and emotional stability.

It allows long-term investments to stay long term.

---

## Mistake 2: Chasing Recent Winners

Beginners often buy what has recently performed well.

This feels logical because the investment looks successful.

But recent performance may already be reflected in the price.

Chasing winners can lead to buying high.

A good investor asks whether the future return still justifies the current price.

---

## Mistake 3: Ignoring Fees

Fees may look small, but they reduce returns every year.

High fund costs, frequent trading costs, and wide bid-ask spreads can quietly damage long-term results.

Beginners should understand what they are paying and why.

Low cost is not everything, but unnecessary cost should be avoided.

---

## Mistake 4: Lack of Diversification

Some beginners put most of their money into one stock, one sector, or one country.

This can work for a while, but it creates heavy dependence on one outcome.

Diversification may feel less exciting, but it helps a portfolio survive uncertainty.

For many beginners, broad ETFs are a practical starting point.

---

## Mistake 5: Checking Prices Too Often

Constantly checking prices can make normal volatility feel like a crisis.

It also increases the chance of emotional decisions.

Long-term investing does not require watching every price movement.

A portfolio should be reviewed regularly, but not obsessively.

---

## Mistake 6: Changing Strategy Too Often

Some investors change strategy whenever the market changes.

They buy growth stocks, then value stocks, then sector ETFs, then cash, then something else.

This prevents any strategy from having time to work.

A good strategy needs patience.

Adjustment is sometimes necessary, but constant change usually reflects emotion.

---

## Lessons From Experienced Investors

Experienced investors often focus on process.

They accept that not every decision will be perfect.

They care about risk, cost, diversification, and behavior.

They know that avoiding major mistakes can be more important than finding brilliant opportunities.

For beginners, this is encouraging.

You do not need to be perfect.

You need a sensible plan and the discipline to avoid harmful habits.

---

## Summary

Common beginner mistakes include investing without emergency savings, chasing recent winners, ignoring fees, lacking diversification, checking prices too often, and changing strategy too often.

These mistakes usually come from normal human emotions.

They can be reduced with planning, patience, and simple rules.

In the next lesson, we will use these lessons to build a personal investment philosophy.
