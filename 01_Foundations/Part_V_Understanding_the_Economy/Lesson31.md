# Lesson 31 — Economic Cycles

> *"Economies do not move in a straight line."*

---

# Introduction

Companies do not operate in empty space.

They sell products, hire workers, borrow money, and compete inside the economy.

The economy grows and slows in cycles.

These cycles influence company profits, investor confidence, interest rates, and market prices.

Beginners do not need to predict every cycle.

But they should understand why markets often behave differently in different economic environments.

---

## Expansion

Expansion is a period when the economy is growing.

Businesses sell more goods and services.

Employment improves.

Consumers may spend more confidently.

Companies may invest in new factories, technology, or employees.

During expansions, corporate profits often improve.

Stock markets may perform well because investors expect future growth.

However, if optimism becomes too strong, prices can rise faster than business reality.

This can create risk later.

---

## Peak

A peak is the late stage of an expansion.

The economy may still look strong, but growth begins to slow.

Inflation may become a concern.

Labor and materials may become more expensive.

Central banks may raise interest rates to cool the economy.

At this stage, investors may still feel confident because recent results have been good.

But markets often look forward.

If investors begin to expect slower growth, stock prices may become more volatile.

---

## Recession

A recession is a period when economic activity declines.

Companies may sell less.

Profits may fall.

Unemployment may rise.

Consumers may reduce spending.

Investors often become fearful during recessions.

Stock markets may fall before or during the downturn.

For long-term investors, recessions are uncomfortable but normal.

They are part of the economic cycle, not proof that investing has stopped working.

---

## Recovery

Recovery begins when the economy starts to improve after a recession or slowdown.

Companies may stabilize.

Consumers may regain confidence.

Central banks may keep interest rates low to support growth.

Stock markets sometimes recover before the economic news looks clearly good.

This happens because markets often react to expectations, not only current conditions.

By the time everything feels safe, prices may already have moved.

This is one reason market timing is difficult.

---

## Summary

Economic cycles include expansion, peak, recession, and recovery.

Each stage affects companies, consumers, interest rates, and investor psychology.

Long-term investors do not need to predict every turn of the cycle.

But they should understand that weak periods are normal.

A diversified portfolio and a long-term plan can help investors stay calm through different environments.

In the next lesson, we will study interest rates, one of the most important forces shaping every stage of the economic cycle.
