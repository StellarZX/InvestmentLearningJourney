# Lesson 32 — Interest Rates

> *"The price of money influences the price of almost everything else."*

---

# Introduction

Interest rates affect almost every part of the financial system.

They influence bank deposits, mortgages, business loans, bond prices, stock valuations, and currency movements.

When financial news talks about markets reacting to central banks, it is often talking about interest rates.

For beginners, interest rates may seem abstract.

But the basic idea is simple:

an interest rate is the cost of borrowing money and the reward for lending money.

---

## Why Central Banks Raise Interest Rates

Central banks often raise interest rates when inflation is too high or the economy is overheating.

Higher interest rates make borrowing more expensive.

Companies may reduce investment.

Consumers may borrow and spend less.

This can slow demand and help reduce inflation pressure.

However, higher rates can also slow economic growth.

That is why interest rate decisions are difficult.

Central banks try to balance price stability with economic activity.

---

## Why Stock Markets React

Stock markets react to interest rates for several reasons.

First, higher rates can reduce company profits because borrowing becomes more expensive.

Second, higher rates can make future profits less valuable today.

This is especially important for growth companies whose expected profits are far in the future.

Third, higher rates can make bonds and cash more attractive compared with stocks.

When safer assets offer better returns, some investors may reduce stock exposure.

This is why stock markets often pay close attention to interest rate expectations.

---

## Stocks and Bonds

Interest rates also strongly affect bonds.

When interest rates rise, existing bond prices usually fall.

This happens because new bonds offer higher yields, so older bonds with lower yields become less attractive.

When interest rates fall, existing bond prices often rise.

Stocks and bonds can respond differently to the same interest rate change.

Sometimes bonds help balance stock risk.

At other times, both stocks and bonds may fall together, especially when inflation is high.

Investors should understand that bonds are more stable than stocks in many cases, but they are not risk-free.

---

## Interest Rates and Long-Term Investors

Long-term investors should not try to predict every interest rate move.

But they should understand why rates matter.

High rates can create pressure on stock valuations and economic growth.

Low rates can support borrowing, spending, and asset prices.

Interest rates also affect the return investors can earn from cash and bonds.

This means asset allocation may feel different in different rate environments.

Still, a long-term plan should not change every time a central bank speaks.

Rates are important, but they are only one part of the investment picture.

---

## Summary

Interest rates are the cost of borrowing and the reward for lending.

Central banks may raise rates to fight inflation or cool an overheated economy.

Stock markets react because rates affect profits, valuations, and competition from bonds and cash.

Bond prices usually move in the opposite direction of interest rates.
