# Lesson 03 — What Is a Stock Exchange?

> *"A stock exchange is not a place where companies become valuable. It is a place where investors trade ownership of valuable companies."*

---

# Introduction

Imagine that you want to buy shares of Apple.

Who should you buy them from?

Should you call Apple directly?

Should you visit its headquarters?

Should you send an email to the CEO?

Of course not.

When people buy or sell stocks, they do not trade directly with the company. Instead, they trade with other investors in a financial marketplace called a **stock exchange**.

Without stock exchanges, buying and selling stocks would be slow, expensive, and unreliable.

Today, every major economy has one or more stock exchanges that connect millions of buyers and sellers every day.

---

# Why Do Stocks Need a Stock Exchange?

Suppose you own shares of a company.

After several years, you decide to sell them.

How can you find someone willing to buy?

Now imagine millions of people asking the same question every day.

Without a central marketplace, every buyer would need to search for a seller individually.

The process would be extremely inefficient.

A stock exchange solves this problem.

It provides a transparent and organized marketplace where investors can trade stocks quickly and fairly.

Instead of negotiating with individual buyers or sellers, investors simply place orders through their brokers.

The exchange matches buyers and sellers automatically.

---

# The Primary Market and the Secondary Market

Many beginners believe they are buying stocks directly from companies.

Most of the time, this is not true.

There are two different markets.

### The Primary Market

The primary market is where a company sells its shares to investors for the first time.

This usually happens during an **Initial Public Offering (IPO)**.

The money raised goes directly to the company.

The company can then use this money to expand its business.

### The Secondary Market

After the IPO, investors begin trading shares with each other.

This is called the secondary market.

When you buy Apple stock today,

Apple does not receive your money.

Instead,

you are buying shares from another investor.

Almost all stock trading that ordinary investors participate in takes place in the secondary market.

---

# How Does a Stock Exchange Work?

A stock exchange itself does not buy or sell stocks.

Its main responsibility is to match buyers and sellers.

Imagine the following situation.

Investor A wants to sell one share of Apple for 200 USD.

Investor B wants to buy one share of Apple for 200 USD.

The exchange automatically matches these two orders.

Once the transaction is completed,

ownership of the share moves from Investor A to Investor B.

The exchange makes this process fast, transparent, and secure.

Modern stock exchanges complete millions of transactions every day within fractions of a second.

---

# The World's Major Stock Exchanges

Every country has its own financial markets.

Some exchanges are much larger and more influential than others.

The four exchanges that beginners should know are listed below.

---

## New York Stock Exchange (NYSE)

The New York Stock Exchange is the largest stock exchange in the world by market capitalization.

Founded in 1792,

it is also one of the oldest stock exchanges.

Many well-known companies are listed on the NYSE, including:

- Coca-Cola
- Walmart
- JPMorgan Chase
- McDonald's

Companies listed on the NYSE are often large and well established.

---

## NASDAQ

NASDAQ was founded in 1971.

Unlike the NYSE,

NASDAQ was designed as an electronic trading market from the beginning.

It is famous for technology companies.

Many of the world's most influential technology companies are listed on NASDAQ, including:

- Apple
- Microsoft
- NVIDIA
- Amazon
- Meta
- Tesla

Although people often associate NASDAQ with technology,

companies from many industries are also listed there.

---

## Shanghai Stock Exchange (SSE)

The Shanghai Stock Exchange is one of the two major stock exchanges in mainland China.

It was established in 1990.

Many large state-owned enterprises and financial institutions are listed in Shanghai.

The SSE is home to numerous blue-chip companies that play important roles in China's economy.

---

## Shenzhen Stock Exchange (SZSE)

The Shenzhen Stock Exchange was also established in 1990.

Compared with Shanghai,

Shenzhen is generally known for having more private companies, technology firms, and high-growth businesses.

Many innovative Chinese companies choose to list in Shenzhen.

---

# Why Are There Different Stock Exchanges?

A common question is:

> Why doesn't every company simply list on the same exchange?

Different exchanges have different listing requirements,

different investor groups,

and different regulatory environments.

For example,

a fast-growing technology company may prefer NASDAQ because many technology investors actively follow that market.

A large traditional company may prefer the NYSE because of its long history and reputation.

Similarly,

Chinese companies may choose Shanghai, Shenzhen, or Hong Kong depending on their business structure, target investors, and regulatory considerations.

---

# Do Investors Need to Choose an Exchange?

For most beginners,

the answer is **no**.

You do not choose an exchange.

You choose a company or an ETF.

Your broker automatically routes your order to the correct exchange.

Understanding the exchange is useful because it helps you understand where companies are listed and how financial markets operate.

However,

your investment decision should focus on the quality of the business rather than the location of its listing.

---

# Summary

A stock exchange is an organized marketplace where investors buy and sell stocks.

Companies use the primary market to raise capital through IPOs.

After that,

most investors trade with each other in the secondary market.

The exchange itself does not own or sell stocks.

Its job is to provide a fair, efficient, and transparent trading environment.

The four stock exchanges introduced in this chapter—NYSE, NASDAQ, Shanghai Stock Exchange, and Shenzhen Stock Exchange—form the foundation of today's global equity markets.

Understanding how stock exchanges work helps us understand how the stock market operates as a whole.

**the market index**.
