# STYLE_GUIDE.md

# Investment Learning Journey Style Guide

This document defines the writing style for the entire book.

Every future lesson should follow these rules.

---

# Overall Goal

The repository is a book, not an online course.

Readers should feel like they are reading a professionally written introductory investment textbook.

The writing should remain consistent throughout all chapters.

---

# Target Audience

Someone who:

- has never invested before;
- has no financial background;
- wants to become a long-term investor.

Assume zero prior knowledge.

---

# English Level

Use simple English.

Target:

CEFR B1–B2.

Avoid unnecessary jargon.

If technical terms appear,

explain them naturally.

---

# Chapter Structure

Every lesson should follow this structure:

```text
Title

Quote

Introduction

Section 1

Section 2

Section 3

...

Summary
```

Do NOT include:

- Learning Objectives
- Quiz
- Homework
- Reflection
- Vocabulary
- Assignments

Those belong outside the textbook.

---

# Writing Style

The tone should be:

- calm
- objective
- educational
- beginner-friendly

Do not sound like a lecture.

Do not sound like marketing.

Do not exaggerate.

Explain ideas naturally.

---

# Educational Philosophy

Always explain:

WHY

↓

WHAT

↓

HOW

For example:

Wrong:

What is an ETF?

↓

How ETFs work.

Correct:

Why investors needed ETFs.

↓

What ETFs are.

↓

How ETFs work.

Readers should naturally ask the next question before it is answered.

---

# Examples

Whenever possible,

use real companies.

Examples:

Apple

Microsoft

Tencent

NVIDIA

Alibaba

S&P 500

CSI 300

Hang Seng Index

Avoid fictional examples unless necessary.

---

# Paragraph Style

Prefer short paragraphs.

Avoid large blocks of text.

One idea per paragraph.

---

# Formatting

Use Markdown.

Use:

# Chapter Title

## Section

No emojis.

No bold for entire paragraphs.

Only emphasize important concepts when necessary.

---

# Ending

Every lesson ends with:

## Summary

The summary should briefly connect the current chapter with the next one.

The reader should naturally want to continue.

---

# What This Book Is NOT

Do not teach:

- technical analysis
- candlestick patterns
- day trading
- stock speculation
- gambling mentality

The entire book promotes:

- long-term investing
- passive investing
- financial literacy
- rational decision making

---

# Consistency

Never contradict previous chapters.

If a later chapter depends on an earlier concept,

briefly remind the reader without repeating the entire explanation.

The whole book should feel like it was written by one author.
