# Lesson 10 — The U.S. Stock Market

> *"When the U.S. market moves, the whole world pays attention."*

---

# Introduction

If you read financial news every day, you will quickly notice something interesting.

No matter where you live,

whether in China,

Europe,

or Japan,

news headlines often begin with:

- "The S&P 500 reached a new high."
- "NASDAQ fell sharply."
- "Wall Street rallied today."

Why does the whole world care so much about the U.S. stock market?

The answer is simple.

The United States has the largest, most influential, and most liquid stock market in the world.

Understanding the U.S. market is essential for every investor, even if you never buy an American stock.

---

# Why Is the U.S. Market the Largest?

The United States has the world's largest economy and many of the world's most valuable companies.

Technology,

healthcare,

consumer products,

finance,

energy,

and artificial intelligence are all strongly represented.

Many companies that people use every day are American.

For example:

- Apple
- Microsoft
- NVIDIA
- Amazon
- Google
- Meta
- Tesla

These companies are not only leaders in the United States.

They influence businesses and consumers around the world.

As a result,

billions of dollars flow into the U.S. stock market every day.

Today,

the U.S. market accounts for a large share of the total value of global stock markets.

---

# Wall Street

When people talk about the U.S. stock market,

they often use the term **Wall Street**.

Wall Street is a street located in New York City.

Historically,

many major financial institutions were headquartered there.

Over time,

"Wall Street" became a symbol of the entire American financial industry.

Today,

when the news says,

> "Wall Street closed higher today,"

it usually means that the U.S. stock market performed well,

not just the businesses located on that street.

---

# The New York Stock Exchange (NYSE)

The New York Stock Exchange (NYSE) is the largest stock exchange in the world.

Founded in 1792,

it has more than two centuries of history.

Many of the world's largest and most established companies are listed on the NYSE.

Examples include:

- Coca-Cola
- Walmart
- JPMorgan Chase
- McDonald's
- Visa

Companies listed on the NYSE are often mature businesses with long operating histories.

For many companies,

being listed on the NYSE is considered a symbol of stability and reputation.

---

# NASDAQ

NASDAQ was established much later, in 1971.

Unlike the NYSE,

NASDAQ was designed as a fully electronic exchange from the beginning.

Today,

NASDAQ is famous for technology and innovation.

Many of the world's leading technology companies are listed there, including:

- Apple
- Microsoft
- NVIDIA
- Amazon
- Meta
- Tesla

Because technology companies often grow rapidly,

NASDAQ is generally more volatile than the NYSE.

When artificial intelligence becomes popular,

or when technology stocks decline,

NASDAQ usually reacts more strongly than other markets.

---

# The S&P 500

Among all American market indices,

the **S&P 500** is the most widely followed.

It includes approximately 500 of the largest publicly traded companies in the United States.

Although it contains only around 500 companies,

they represent a large proportion of the total value of the U.S. stock market.

For this reason,

many investors consider the S&P 500 to be the best indicator of the overall American stock market.

When investors say,

> "The U.S. market gained 1% today,"

they are often referring to the S&P 500.

Many long-term investors choose to invest in S&P 500 ETFs because they provide exposure to hundreds of high-quality companies through a single investment.

---

# Why Does the Whole World Care About the U.S. Market?

The influence of the U.S. market extends far beyond the United States.

Many pension funds,

insurance companies,

banks,

and investment funds around the world own American stocks.

In addition,

many international companies rely on the U.S. economy.

As a result,

when the U.S. market experiences significant gains or losses,

investors in many other countries often react as well.

For example,

a sharp decline in the S&P 500 may affect stock markets in Europe and Asia on the following trading day.

This is why financial news around the world closely follows the performance of the U.S. market.

---

# Should Beginners Invest in the U.S. Market?

Many beginners wonder whether they should invest in China or the United States.

There is no single correct answer.

The Chinese market provides exposure to China's long-term economic development.

The U.S. market offers access to many of the world's largest and most innovative companies.

Many experienced investors choose not to select one market over the other.

Instead,

they build diversified portfolios that include investments from multiple countries.

As we will learn later,

diversification is one of the most important principles of long-term investing.

---

# Summary

The United States has the world's largest and most influential stock market.

Its two major stock exchanges, the New York Stock Exchange and NASDAQ, are home to many of the world's leading companies.

Among American market indices, the S&P 500 is the most widely used measure of overall market performance.

Because the U.S. economy plays a central role in the global financial system, movements in the U.S. stock market often influence markets around the world.

Understanding the U.S. market helps investors better understand global investing, even if they choose to invest primarily in their own country.

**bull markets and bear markets**.
