# Lesson 22 — How ETFs Work

> *"An ETF is simple to use, but there is a careful structure behind it."*

---

# Introduction

Earlier, we learned that ETFs can help beginners invest in a diversified way.

Now we will look more closely at how ETFs work.

An ETF, or exchange-traded fund, is a fund that trades on a stock exchange like a stock.

But inside, it usually holds a basket of assets, such as stocks or bonds.

Many ETFs are designed to track an index.

The goal is not to beat the index.

The goal is to follow it closely.

---

## Why ETF Structure Matters

Beginners often see only the market price of an ETF.

They buy and sell it through a brokerage account.

This makes an ETF feel similar to a stock.

But an ETF is different because it represents ownership in a fund.

The fund owns many securities.

If the ETF tracks an index, the fund manager builds a portfolio that looks like that index.

This structure allows one ETF share to give investors exposure to many companies.

That is why ETFs are useful for diversification.

---

## Tracking an Index

An index is a rule-based list of securities.

The S&P 500 includes large U.S. companies.

The CSI 300 includes major A-share companies in China.

An ETF that tracks an index tries to match the index's performance.

Some ETFs hold every security in the index.

This is called full replication.

Other ETFs hold a sample of securities that are expected to behave like the index.

This is called sampling.

---

## Creation and Redemption

ETFs have a special mechanism called creation and redemption.

Large financial institutions, often called authorized participants, can create new ETF shares or redeem existing shares.

When demand for an ETF rises, new shares can be created.

When demand falls, shares can be removed.

This mechanism helps keep the ETF's market price close to the value of the assets it owns.

That value is called net asset value, or NAV.

---

## ETF Price and Fund Value

An ETF has a market price and a fund value.

The market price is what investors pay on the exchange.

The fund value is based on the assets inside the ETF.

Usually, these two numbers are close.

But they may differ slightly, especially in fast-moving markets or less liquid ETFs.

For broad, heavily traded ETFs, the difference is usually small.

For narrow or less active ETFs, it may be more important.

---

## Summary

An ETF is a fund that trades on an exchange.

Many ETFs track an index by holding the securities in that index or a representative sample.

The creation and redemption mechanism helps keep the ETF price close to the value of its holdings.

ETFs are easy to buy, but their structure is more than a simple stock trade.

In the next lesson, we will study expense ratios.

Even small fees can matter a lot when money is invested for many years.
