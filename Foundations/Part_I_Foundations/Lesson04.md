# Lesson 04 — What Is a Market Index?

> *"A market index does not tell you how one company performs. It tells you how the market performs."*

---

# Introduction

Imagine you want to know how the Chinese stock market performed today.

There are more than 5,000 listed companies in China.

Would you check every company's stock price?

Of course not.

Even if you spent only one minute on each company,

it would take several days.

Investors need a simple way to understand the overall market.

This is why market indices were created.

A market index summarizes the performance of a selected group of stocks into a single number.

Instead of looking at thousands of companies,

we only need to look at one index.

---

# What Is a Market Index?

A market index measures the overall performance of a group of stocks.

It is not a company.

It is not a stock.

It is not an investment product.

Instead,

it is simply a statistical indicator.

Think of it as the "average report card" for a group of companies.

If most companies in the group perform well,

the index rises.

If most companies perform poorly,

the index falls.

For example,

when people say,

> "The market rose by 2% today."

they usually mean

> "A market index rose by 2%."

---

# Why Do We Need Market Indices?

Without indices,

understanding the market would be almost impossible.

Imagine watching a football league.

You do not watch every player's statistics before knowing who is winning.

Instead,

you first look at the league table.

The league table gives you a quick overview.

A market index plays the same role in investing.

It helps investors answer questions such as:

- Is the market rising or falling?
- Which country's market is performing better?
- Is this year better than last year?

Without market indices,

comparing markets would be extremely difficult.

---

# How Is a Market Index Constructed?

An index is created by selecting a group of representative companies.

Different indices use different selection rules.

Some include only the largest companies.

Others focus on technology companies.

Some represent an entire country's stock market.

Each company contributes differently to the index.

Larger companies usually have a greater influence than smaller ones.

For this reason,

when a very large company moves sharply,

the entire index may also move significantly.

---

# Major Market Indices

There are hundreds of market indices around the world.

However,

beginners only need to know a few of the most important ones.

---

## CSI 300

The CSI 300 Index represents 300 of the largest and most liquid companies listed on the Shanghai and Shenzhen stock exchanges.

It is widely regarded as one of the most important indicators of the Chinese A-share market.

Many investors use the CSI 300 to evaluate the overall performance of China's stock market.

If someone says,

> "The Chinese market fell today,"

they are often referring to the CSI 300.

---

## S&P 500

The S&P 500 is one of the most famous market indices in the world.

It contains approximately 500 large companies in the United States.

Many globally recognized companies are included,

such as:

- Apple
- Microsoft
- Amazon
- NVIDIA

Although the S&P 500 contains only around 500 companies,

it represents a large proportion of the total value of the U.S. stock market.

Because of this,

many investors consider it the best indicator of the overall U.S. market.

---

## NASDAQ-100

The NASDAQ-100 tracks 100 large non-financial companies listed on the NASDAQ exchange.

It is particularly well known for its strong concentration of technology companies.

Examples include:

- Apple
- Microsoft
- NVIDIA
- Meta
- Amazon

Compared with the S&P 500,

the NASDAQ-100 is generally more volatile because technology companies often experience larger price fluctuations.

---

## Hang Seng Index

The Hang Seng Index is one of the most important market indices in Hong Kong.

It includes many of the largest companies listed on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange.

Examples include:

- Tencent
- HSBC
- AIA
- China Mobile

Because many Chinese companies are listed in Hong Kong,

the Hang Seng Index is also closely watched by international investors interested in China's economy.

---

# Why Should Beginners Focus on Indices Instead of Individual Stocks?

Many beginners immediately start searching for the "best stock."

Professional investors often begin somewhere else.

They first ask:

> "How is the overall market performing?"

This is because individual stocks are strongly influenced by the broader market.

If the entire market is falling sharply,

even excellent companies may experience declining stock prices.

Understanding the market first makes it easier to understand individual companies later.

---

# What a Market Index Cannot Tell You

Although indices are very useful,

they have limitations.

An index cannot tell you:

- whether a specific company is a good investment;
- whether a stock is expensive or cheap;
- what will happen tomorrow.

An index only summarizes the overall performance of a group of companies.

It is a tool for observation,

not prediction.

---

# Summary

A market index is a statistical measure of the performance of a group of stocks.

It provides investors with a simple way to understand the overall market.

Different indices represent different markets and different groups of companies.

Among the most important indices are the CSI 300, the S&P 500, the NASDAQ-100, and the Hang Seng Index.

Learning to follow market indices is one of the first steps toward becoming a long-term investor.
