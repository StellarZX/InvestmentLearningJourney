# Lesson 07 — The Investment Framework

> *"Knowledge becomes useful only when individual concepts are connected together."*

---

# Introduction

During the past six lessons, we have learned many new concepts.

At first glance, they may appear to be independent topics.

However, they actually describe one complete investment system.

Before moving on to financial markets around the world, let us connect everything we have learned into one clear picture.

---

# Step 1 — Why Do We Invest?

Everything begins with a simple question.

Why should we invest?

The answer is not to become rich overnight.

The purpose of investing is to preserve and grow wealth over the long term.

Saving protects our money.

Investing helps our money grow faster than inflation.

---

# Step 2 — What Are We Buying?

Investing is not simply buying numbers on a screen.

A stock represents ownership in a real business.

When we buy a stock,

we become one of the company's shareholders.

Therefore,

investing always begins with businesses.

---

# Step 3 — Where Do Stocks Trade?

If investors want to buy and sell stocks,

they need a marketplace.

That marketplace is called a stock exchange.

Stock exchanges make trading efficient,

transparent,

and secure.

Examples include:

- NYSE
- NASDAQ
- Shanghai Stock Exchange
- Shenzhen Stock Exchange

---

# Step 4 — How Do We Understand the Market?

Thousands of companies trade every day.

Following each company individually would be impossible.

Market indices summarize the performance of many companies into one number.

Instead of watching thousands of stocks,

investors often begin by observing market indices.

Examples include:

- CSI 300
- S&P 500
- NASDAQ-100
- Hang Seng Index

---

# Step 5 — How Can We Invest in an Index?

An index cannot be bought directly.

This is why ETFs exist.

An ETF tracks the performance of an index,

allowing ordinary investors to invest in hundreds of companies through a single product.

---

# Step 6 — Two Different Investment Philosophies

Once investors understand ETFs,

they naturally encounter two major investment approaches.

Active investing attempts to outperform the market.

Passive investing attempts to follow the market.

Although both approaches have advantages,

this book mainly focuses on passive investing because it provides a simple,

low-cost,

and practical approach for long-term investors.

---

# The Complete Picture

The relationship among all the concepts introduced in Part I can be summarized as follows.

```

Business

↓

↓

↓

↓

```

Everything we learn in the following chapters will build upon this framework.

In Part I, we did not learn how to make money quickly.
