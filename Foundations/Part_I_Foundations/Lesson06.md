# Lesson 06 — Mutual Funds vs. ETFs

> *"An investment product is only a tool. What really matters is the investment philosophy behind it."*

---

# Introduction

By now, we have learned about stocks, stock exchanges, market indices, and ETFs.

At this point, many beginners ask another important question:

> "If ETFs are funds, what are the other types of funds?"

The answer is **mutual funds**.

In many countries, including China, mutual funds have existed for decades.

In fact, many people buy mutual funds through banks or mobile apps without fully understanding how they work.

Both mutual funds and ETFs allow investors to own a diversified portfolio with a single purchase.

However, they are not exactly the same.

More importantly,

they often represent two different investment philosophies:

**active investing** and **passive investing**.

---

# Active Investing

Active investing means trying to outperform the market.

Instead of simply following an index,

a professional fund manager actively decides:

- which companies to buy,
- which companies to sell,
- when to increase positions,
- and when to reduce risk.

The goal is simple:

> **Beat the market.**

For example,

if the CSI 300 returns 8% this year,

an actively managed fund hopes to earn 10%, 12%, or even more.

To achieve this,

fund managers spend significant resources researching companies,

meeting management teams,

analyzing financial statements,

and predicting future trends.

Active investing requires experience,

continuous research,

and higher management costs.

---

# Passive Investing

Passive investing takes a completely different approach.

Instead of asking,

> "Which company will perform best?"

passive investors ask,

> "Why not own the entire market?"

Rather than trying to beat an index,

passive investing simply follows it.

If the market grows,

the investment grows.

If the market declines,

the investment also declines.

Most ETFs are examples of passive investing.

Their objective is not to outperform the market,

but to match the market as closely as possible.

---

# What Is a Mutual Fund?

A mutual fund pools money from many investors into one large investment portfolio.

Professional fund managers decide how the money is invested.

Some mutual funds focus on technology,

some focus on healthcare,

and others invest across many industries.

Many mutual funds are actively managed,

although passive mutual funds also exist.

Unlike ETFs,

traditional mutual funds are usually bought and sold through the fund company.

Their prices are calculated only once each trading day,

based on the fund's **Net Asset Value (NAV)**.

---

# What Is an ETF?

An ETF is also an investment fund.

The main difference is that it trades on a stock exchange just like a stock.

You can buy or sell an ETF at any time during market hours.

Most ETFs are designed to track a market index,

making them passive investment products.

Because ETFs usually require less active management,

their management fees are often lower than those of actively managed mutual funds.

---

# Mutual Funds vs. ETFs

Although mutual funds and ETFs are similar in many ways,

they have several important differences.

| Mutual Fund | ETF |
|--------------|-----|
| Usually actively managed | Usually passively managed |
| Bought from the fund company | Traded on a stock exchange |
| Priced once per day | Trades throughout the day |
| Often higher management fees | Usually lower management fees |
| Attempts to beat the market | Attempts to follow the market |

It is important to remember that these are general characteristics.

Some ETFs are actively managed,

and some mutual funds track indices.

However,

most investment products follow the pattern above.

---

# Which One Is Better?

Many beginners ask:

> "Should I buy mutual funds or ETFs?"

There is no universal answer.

If you believe professional managers can consistently outperform the market,

you may prefer actively managed mutual funds.

If you believe that consistently beating the market is extremely difficult,

you may prefer passive investing through ETFs.

Over the past several decades,

passive investing has become increasingly popular around the world.

Many investors appreciate its simplicity,

low cost,

and long-term consistency.

This is also the investment philosophy that this book will mainly introduce.

---

# Summary

Both mutual funds and ETFs allow investors to own diversified portfolios.

The biggest difference is not the product itself,

but the investment philosophy behind it.

Active investing attempts to outperform the market.

Passive investing attempts to follow the market.

Most mutual funds are actively managed,

while most ETFs are designed to track market indices.

Neither approach is perfect.

However,

understanding the difference between active and passive investing is one of the most important steps toward becoming a thoughtful investor.
