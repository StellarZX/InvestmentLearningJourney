# Lesson 02 — What Is a Stock?

> *"When you buy a stock, you are buying a small piece of a real business."*

---

# Learning Objectives

After completing this lesson, you should be able to:

- Understand what a stock really represents.
- Explain why companies issue stocks.
- Understand why investors buy stocks.
- Distinguish between a company and its stock.
- Understand the two ways investors make money from stocks.

---

# Introduction

Many beginners think that a stock is simply a number that goes up and down every day.

However, a stock is much more than a price on a screen.

A stock represents ownership in a real company.

Every stock you buy means that you own a very small part of a business.

Understanding this idea is the foundation of investing.

---

# 1. What Is a Stock?

A stock (also called a **share** or **equity**) represents ownership in a company.

Imagine a company is like a large pizza.

The pizza can be divided into many slices.

Each slice represents one share.

If you own one slice,

you own a small part of the whole pizza.

Similarly,

if a company has one million shares,

and you own one hundred shares,

you own a very small part of that company.

Even though your ownership is tiny,

you are still one of its shareholders.

---

# 2. Why Do Companies Sell Stocks?

Running a business requires money.

A company may need money to:

- build new factories
- develop new technology
- hire more employees
- expand into new countries

Instead of borrowing money from a bank,

a company can sell part of its ownership to the public.

This process is called **issuing shares**.

The company receives money.

Investors become shareholders.

Both sides benefit.

---

# 3. Why Do Investors Buy Stocks?

People buy stocks because they expect the company to become more valuable in the future.

There are two main ways investors can make money.

## Capital Gain

Suppose you buy one share for **100 RMB**.

Several years later,

the price rises to **160 RMB**.

If you sell it,

your capital gain is **60 RMB**.

---

## Dividends

Some companies share part of their profits with shareholders.

This payment is called a **dividend**.

For example,

if a company pays a dividend of 2 RMB per share,

and you own 100 shares,

you receive:

2 × 100 = 200 RMB.

Not every company pays dividends.

Many fast-growing companies prefer to reinvest their profits into future growth.

---

# 4. Company vs Stock

This is one of the most important concepts in investing.

A company and its stock are not the same thing.

| Company | Stock |
|----------|-------|
| A real business | Ownership of the business |
| Produces products or services | Can be bought or sold |
| Operates every day | Price changes every trading day |

For example,

Apple is a company.

Apple shares are stocks.

Apple designs products such as the iPhone and Mac.

Its stock represents ownership in that business.

---

# 5. Why Do Stock Prices Change?

Many beginners believe that stock prices only depend on company profits.

In reality,

stock prices are determined by **supply and demand**.

If more people want to buy,

the price rises.

If more people want to sell,

the price falls.

Company performance is important,

but expectations are equally important.

Sometimes,

a company reports excellent results,

yet its stock price still falls.

Why?

Because investors expected even better results.

The market always looks forward,

not backward.

---

# 6. A Good Company Is Not Always a Good Investment

This idea surprises many beginners.

A company can be excellent,

but its stock may still perform poorly.

Imagine two situations.

Company A is already extremely popular.

Everyone expects it to grow rapidly.

Company B is less popular,

but its business is improving quickly.

Sometimes,

Company B's stock performs better,

because expectations were lower.

Therefore,

a good company is not automatically a good investment.

The price you pay also matters.

---

# 7. Common Misunderstandings

### ❌ A stock is just a number on a screen.

✅ A stock represents ownership in a real business.

---

### ❌ A good company always has a rising stock price.

✅ Stock prices depend on both business performance and investor expectations.

---

### ❌ The stock market is only for rich people.

✅ Anyone can become a shareholder by purchasing stocks or ETFs.

---

### ❌ Every company pays dividends.

✅ Some companies pay dividends, while others reinvest all of their profits.

---

# Thinking Like an Investor

When you see a stock price,

do not ask:

> "Will this number go up tomorrow?"

Instead, ask:

> "What kind of business does this company own?"

Professional investors spend more time understanding businesses than predicting prices.

This way of thinking is one of the biggest differences between investing and gambling.

---

# Key Takeaways

- A stock represents ownership in a company.
- Companies issue stocks to raise capital.
- Investors make money through capital gains and dividends.
- A company and its stock are different concepts.
- Stock prices are determined by supply and demand.
- A great company is not always a great investment.
