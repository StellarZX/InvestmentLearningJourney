# Lesson 36 — Fear & Greed

> *"Markets move with numbers, but investors move with emotions."*

---

# Introduction

In the previous lesson, we learned that psychology affects investment decisions.

Two emotions appear again and again in financial markets:

fear and greed.

Fear often appears when prices fall.

Greed often appears when prices rise.

Both emotions are natural.

But if they control decisions, they can damage long-term returns.

The purpose of this lesson is not to judge emotions.

It is to understand them.

---

## Emotional Investing

Emotional investing happens when decisions are driven mainly by feelings instead of a clear plan.

An investor may buy because prices are rising and the future feels exciting.

The same investor may sell later because prices are falling and the future feels dangerous.

This pattern is common.

It often leads to buying high and selling low.

The investor is not stupid.

The investor is human.

Markets are designed in a way that constantly tests patience.

---

## Fear

Fear is strongest during market declines.

Headlines become negative.

Portfolio values fall.

People begin to question whether investing works at all.

Fear can be useful if it reminds investors to manage risk.

But fear becomes harmful when it creates panic selling.

Selling during a temporary decline may turn paper losses into permanent losses.

Before selling, a long-term investor should ask:

has my plan changed, or only the price?

---

## Greed

Greed is strongest during market booms.

Investors see others making money and want to join quickly.

They may ignore valuation, risk, and diversification.

They may believe that a rising asset will keep rising forever.

Greed can make risky investments look safe because recent returns are strong.

This is dangerous.

The more popular an investment becomes, the more carefully investors should examine what they are paying for.

Good past returns do not guarantee good future returns.

---

## Market Sentiment

Market sentiment means the overall mood of investors.

When sentiment is positive, investors are willing to take more risk.

When sentiment is negative, investors become cautious.

Sentiment can move prices far away from business reality, at least for a time.

This is why markets can become too optimistic or too pessimistic.

Long-term investors do not need to fight every wave of sentiment.

But they should know when their own emotions are being influenced by the crowd.

---

## Staying Rational

Staying rational does not mean having no emotions.

It means making important decisions before emotions become intense.

A written investment plan helps.

Asset allocation helps.

Diversification helps.

Regular investing helps.

Reviewing a portfolio on a schedule, instead of checking prices constantly, can also help.

The goal is to create distance between market noise and personal decisions.

---

## Summary

Fear and greed are normal emotions in investing.

Fear can lead to panic selling.

Greed can lead to chasing expensive assets.

Market sentiment can influence prices and investor behavior.

Long-term investors manage these emotions with a clear plan, diversification, and disciplined habits.

In the next lesson, we will study FOMO, a common form of greed that pushes investors to chase what has already become popular.
