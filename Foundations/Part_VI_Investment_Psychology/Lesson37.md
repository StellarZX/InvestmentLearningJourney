# Lesson 37 — FOMO

> *"The fear of missing out can make investors forget their own plan."*

---

# Introduction

FOMO means fear of missing out.

It appears when investors see others making money and feel pressure to join.

This feeling is especially strong when one stock, one sector, or one theme rises quickly.

People talk about it online.

News reports celebrate it.

Friends may say they already bought it.

The beginner starts to feel that waiting is a mistake.

FOMO is powerful because it combines greed, social pressure, and fear.

---

## Why FOMO Happens

FOMO happens because people compare themselves with others.

If your portfolio grows slowly while someone else says they doubled their money, it is easy to feel left behind.

The problem is that we usually see other people's successes more clearly than their risks.

People often talk about gains.

They may not talk about losses.

They may not explain how much risk they took.

They may not know whether their success came from skill or luck.

Investing decisions should not be based on someone else's highlight story.

---

## Chasing Hot Investments

Chasing means buying an investment mainly because its price has already risen a lot.

Sometimes the price continues to rise.

But often, late buyers enter when expectations are already very high.

If the story becomes less exciting, the price can fall sharply.

Hot investments can appear in many forms:

technology stocks, new energy stocks, cryptocurrencies, artificial intelligence themes, or any popular market story.

The topic may be real and important.

But a real trend can still become too expensive.

---

## The Difference Between Opportunity and Pressure

Not every popular investment is bad.

Some major trends are real and long-lasting.

The problem is not popularity itself.

The problem is buying without understanding.

A true opportunity can be explained calmly.

An emotional purchase often feels urgent.

If the main reason for buying is "everyone else is making money," the decision is probably driven by pressure.

Long-term investors should slow down when they feel urgency.

Urgency often belongs to emotion, not analysis.

---

## Building Discipline

Discipline means following a plan even when emotions are loud.

A disciplined investor defines asset allocation before markets become exciting.

They decide how much risk they are willing to take.

They avoid putting too much money into one theme.

They understand that missing one opportunity is not the same as failing as an investor.

There will always be another popular story.

The goal is not to catch every trend.

The goal is to build wealth without destroying the plan.

---

## Summary

FOMO is the fear of missing out on gains that others seem to be enjoying.

It can push investors to chase hot investments without understanding risk or valuation.

A popular trend may be real, but the price may still be too high.

Long-term investors fight FOMO with discipline, asset allocation, and clear rules.
