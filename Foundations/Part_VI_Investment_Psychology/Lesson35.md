# Lesson 35 — Behavioral Finance

# Introduction

So far, we have studied markets, ETFs, risk, portfolios, and the economy.

These topics are important.

But knowledge alone is not enough.

Many investors understand the correct principles and still make poor decisions.

Why?

Because investing is emotional.

Behavioral finance studies how human psychology affects financial decisions.

It helps explain why investors often buy high, sell low, follow the crowd, and ignore their own plans.

---

## Why Humans Make Irrational Decisions

Humans did not evolve to be perfect investors.

We are naturally sensitive to fear, loss, social pressure, and recent events.

These instincts can be useful in daily life.

But in investing, they can create problems.

When markets fall, fear tells us to escape.

When markets rise quickly, excitement tells us to join.

When everyone talks about one hot investment, social pressure tells us not to be left behind.

The market uses prices, but investors use emotions.

This is why self-control matters.

---

## Loss Aversion

Loss aversion means people feel the pain of losses more strongly than the pleasure of gains.

Losing 1,000 RMB often feels worse than gaining 1,000 RMB feels good.

Because of this, investors may sell during a decline simply to stop feeling pain.

They may also refuse to sell a poor investment because selling would make the loss feel real.

Loss aversion can make people focus too much on short-term price movement.

Long-term investors need to accept that temporary losses are normal.

---

## Recency Bias

Recency bias means giving too much importance to what happened recently.

If the market has risen for several months, investors may believe it will keep rising.

If the market has fallen for several months, investors may believe it will never recover.

Recent events feel vivid because they are easy to remember.

But markets move in cycles.

The recent past is not always a good guide to the future.

This is why investors need a written plan.

A plan can help balance emotions created by recent events.

---

## Herd Behavior

Herd behavior means following the crowd.

In investing, this can feel safe.

If many people are buying the same asset, a beginner may think it must be a good idea.

But crowds can be wrong.

During bubbles, many investors buy because others are buying.

During crashes, many investors sell because others are selling.

Following the crowd can make investors buy expensive assets and sell cheap assets.

A long-term investor should listen to information, but not blindly copy behavior.

---

## Summary

Behavioral finance explains why investors often make irrational decisions.

Fear, loss aversion, recency bias, and herd behavior can damage long-term results.

The solution is not to remove emotion completely.

That is impossible.

The solution is to build systems that reduce emotional decision-making.
