# Lesson 20 — Asset Allocation

> *"What you own matters, but how much of each asset you own matters even more."*

---

# Introduction

Diversification teaches us not to depend on one investment.

Asset allocation takes the next step.

It asks how much of a portfolio should be placed in different asset classes, such as stocks, bonds, gold, and cash.

For many long-term investors, asset allocation is more important than choosing individual products.

The mix of assets determines much of the portfolio's risk, return, and emotional difficulty.

---

## Stocks

Stocks represent ownership in companies.

They usually have higher expected returns than cash or bonds over long periods.

But they also move more in price.

Stocks can be suitable for long-term goals because companies can grow with the economy.

However, stock investors must accept market declines.

A portfolio with a high stock allocation may grow more over decades, but it can also fall sharply in difficult years.

---

## Bonds

Bonds are loans to governments or companies.

When you buy a bond, you are usually lending money in exchange for interest payments and repayment at maturity.

Bonds are often more stable than stocks, although they are not risk-free.

Bond prices can fall when interest rates rise.

Companies or governments can also have credit problems.

Still, bonds can help reduce portfolio volatility.

They may provide income and balance when stock markets are weak.

---

## Gold

Gold does not produce profits like a company.

It does not pay interest like a bond.

But some investors use gold as a store of value and a hedge against certain risks.

Gold may perform well during periods of inflation, currency concern, or financial stress.

However, gold can also be volatile and may perform poorly for long periods.

In a portfolio, gold is usually a small supporting asset rather than the main engine of growth.

---

## Cash

Cash includes bank deposits and money market funds.

Cash is important because it provides flexibility.

It can be used for emergencies, daily spending, and near-term goals.

Cash also helps investors avoid selling stocks during a market decline when money is suddenly needed.

But too much cash can reduce long-term growth.

Inflation can slowly reduce its purchasing power.

---

## Building a Balanced Portfolio

A balanced portfolio combines assets with different roles.

Stocks provide long-term growth.

Bonds can add stability.

Gold may offer protection in some environments.

Cash provides flexibility.

There is no single perfect allocation for everyone.

A young investor with a long time horizon may hold more stocks.

An investor close to retirement may prefer more bonds and cash.

The right allocation depends on time horizon, goals, income stability, and emotional comfort.

---

## Summary

Asset allocation is the decision of how much to invest in each asset class.

Stocks, bonds, gold, and cash play different roles.

Stocks can drive long-term growth, while bonds and cash can improve stability.

Gold may help in some uncertain environments.

A good allocation matches the investor's goals and risk tolerance.
